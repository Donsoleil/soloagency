# Niche advantage

If you spent years inside the industry you are now selling to, that is the one
asset a funded competitor cannot buy quickly. Most founders under-use it out of
modesty and then compete on features instead, which is the fight they lose.

## When not to use

If you are an outsider to the market, this skill does not apply and pretending
otherwise reads as fraudulent to the people you are selling to. They can tell
in one sentence.

## The three things expertise actually buys

**Vocabulary.** You know what the work is called. Copy written by an insider
passes a test outsider copy fails in the first line, because you name the
problem the way they name it rather than the way a marketer would.

**Access.** The association, the conference, the group chat, the licensing
body, the regional meetup. These are distribution channels that do not appear
in any paid-acquisition playbook and that competitors cannot buy into. List
yours by name, with the rough size of each, before spending a dollar on ads.

**Judgment about defaults.** You know which of the ten possible features is the
one that matters, because you have done the job. That shortens the build.

## Turning it into distribution

Rank your access points by reach times trust, then work the top two properly
rather than all eight badly. Trust is the multiplier, and it is why one post in
a 200-person professional group beats a cold list of 10,000.

Credibility content means showing the work, not teaching the basics. Publish
the thing only a practitioner could write: the actual numbers, the actual
mistake you made, the template you really use. Beginner content signals you
left the profession. Practitioner content signals you are still in it.

Positioning line: "built by a [role] who [specific credential], for [segment]".
Specific beats impressive. Twelve years doing the exact job beats a vague
seniority claim.

## The trap

Your expertise tells you what the work needs. It does not tell you what other
practitioners will pay for, because you are one person and possibly an unusual
one. Everything here still has to survive `solo-validate`. Expertise shortens
discovery, it does not replace it.

## Evidence

A named list of access points with rough audience size per point, the two you
picked and why, and the positioning line. Unnamed channels ("my network") are
`NOT VERIFIED`.
