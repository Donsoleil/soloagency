# Landing page

One page, one job: a stranger from the segment understands what this is, who
it is for, and what it costs, inside about eight seconds.

## Read first

Pull the customer's own phrasing from `solo-customer-research` before writing a
word. The headline is their sentence about their problem, not your sentence
about your product. If that profile does not exist, write it first, because
without it you will write a headline about yourself.

## Above the fold, four elements

**Headline: the outcome, in their words.** Not the category, not the mechanism.
"Get your invoices out on Friday without opening a spreadsheet" beats
"AI-powered invoicing platform". If your headline would fit your competitor, it
is not a headline, it is a category label.

**Subhead: who it is for and the mechanism, one sentence.** This is where the
segment gets named explicitly. Naming the segment repels the wrong visitor,
which is the point.

**One primary action.** One button, one label, repeated down the page. Two
competing CTAs above the fold halves the strength of both.

**Proof, immediately.** A named customer, a number, a recognizable logo, or an
honest count. Something that says other people already decided.

## Section order below the fold

Problem in their words, so they know you understand it. Then the product doing
the job, shown rather than described, ideally a short clip. Then the two or
three outcomes that matter, each with the objection it answers. Then proof in
volume. Then price, or a clear pointer to it. Then FAQ, which is where you put
the objections you keep hearing on sales calls. Then the CTA again.

Never hide the price. A visitor who cannot find what it costs assumes it is
expensive and leaves, and you never learn they were there.

## What to cut

Anything about your journey as a founder, above the fold. Anything a
competitor could say verbatim. Adjectives doing the work a number should do.
Feature lists longer than three items before the first proof element. A second
navigation menu. The page is shorter than you want it to be.

## The read-aloud test

Read the first two sentences to someone from the segment, out loud, and ask
what they think it does and who it is for. If they hesitate, the headline is
wrong. This test takes two minutes and beats a week of iterating alone.

## The notebook

Read `~/.soloagency/memory.md` before asking the founder anything it already
answers. When this skill produces a decision, write the entry: what was
decided, why, whether it is one-way, the evidence behind it, and
`Outcome: (open)`. `solo-memory` owns the format.

A decision made and not written down is a decision you will make again from
scratch in four months.

## Evidence

The shipped URL, the headline stated as the customer's own phrase with a
pointer to where that phrase came from, and one read-aloud test with what the
person said back. A page nobody outside the build has read is `NOT VERIFIED`.
