# Validate

A validation test is only real if it can fail. Set the number that means no
before you run it, in writing, or you will reinterpret whatever happens as
encouragement.

## The rule that does the work

Write this sentence and keep it: "If fewer than N of M do X by DATE, I stop or
change direction." Then hold to it. Most founders skip this and end up building
anyway, having collected some signups as permission.

## Signal strength, weakest to strongest

1. Verbal enthusiasm. Worth nothing. People are kind.
2. Email signup. Weak. Costs them nothing.
3. Signup plus a specific answer about their situation. Better, it costs effort.
4. Booking a call. Real, it costs time.
5. Card entered, even for a trial. Strong.
6. Money paid before the thing exists. The only signal that fully counts.

Design for the strongest signal you can stomach asking for. Founders default to
level 2 because it is comfortable and then wonder why the list does not convert.

## Tests worth running

**Pre-sale.** Describe the outcome, name the price, take money or a deposit,
promise a date, and honor a refund. The cleanest test there is. If asking for
money feels too early, that discomfort is the test working.

**Fake door.** Real page, real price, real button. The button leads to "not
open yet, here is what happens next" and captures intent. Be honest on the
other side of the click, always.

**Concierge.** Deliver the outcome manually for three to five customers, paid,
before automating anything. Slow, unscalable, and the highest-information test
available, because you learn what the product actually has to do.

**Waitlist with a qualifying question.** Only useful with the question. A bare
email list tells you people can type.

Run one test at a time. Parallel tests on a small audience produce noise you
cannot attribute.

## Reading a failure honestly

No signal has three causes and they need different responses: wrong people
(fix targeting, retest), wrong message (fix the words, retest), or wrong
problem (stop). Founders almost always conclude "wrong message" because it is
the cheapest to fix. Before you accept that, check whether your traffic
actually reached the segment from `solo-customer-research`. A test that reached
the wrong people tested nothing.

## Reversibility

Running the test is reversible. Building six months of product on a passed
test you designed to be unfailable is not.

## Evidence

The written threshold sentence dated before the test, the raw numbers after,
and the resulting call with its reason. Threshold written afterwards means
`NOT VERIFIED`.
