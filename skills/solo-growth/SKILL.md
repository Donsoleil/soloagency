# Growth

One channel, worked properly, beats five channels touched occasionally. As one
person, the constraint is your hours, so the strategy is almost entirely about
what you refuse to do.

## Read first

Pull where existing customers actually came from, by asking them or from
whatever attribution exists. Founders regularly invest in the channel they
enjoy rather than the one that produced customers, and this one read fixes
that.

## Pick one channel

Score each candidate on three things: does your segment actually gather there,
can you do it without a team, and does the effort compound or evaporate.

Compounding channels, meaning content, SEO, community standing, and
partnerships, are slow and then keep paying. Evaporating channels, meaning ads
and cold outreach, work immediately and stop the day you stop. As a solo
founder with limited cash, one compounding channel plus one evaporating channel
for immediate cash flow is the practical shape, and never more than that.

Commit for a real window, meaning a quarter, not a fortnight. Channel
switching before the compounding starts is the most common way solo founders
waste a year.

## The loop, not the funnel

A funnel ends. A loop feeds itself: a customer gets value, that value produces
something visible to the next customer, and that brings the next customer in.
Ask what your product naturally produces that a stranger could see. Output
worth sharing, an invitation to a colleague, a public artifact, a result
someone brags about. If the answer is nothing, there is no loop, and you are
buying every customer forever.

## Activation before acquisition

If new users do not reach first value, more traffic makes the leak bigger. Check
the activation rate from `solo-conversion` before spending anything on
acquisition. This ordering is not optional and it is routinely ignored.

## Referral

Referral works when the product is already loved and the ask is timed to the
moment value landed, not on a schedule. Incentives on a product people merely
tolerate produce fraud and noise. Ask right after something good happened.

## What to stop

Every quarter, name the activity you will stop. Something with no attributable
customers, or that costs hours you cannot spare. Solo founders accumulate
obligations, newsletters, channels, communities, and never prune, and the
pruning is where the hours come from.

## Evidence

Where existing customers actually came from, the single channel chosen with the
three-way score, the loop described in one sentence or explicitly named absent,
the activation rate, and one named activity stopped.
