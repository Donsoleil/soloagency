# Customer research

Most solo founders build for an imagined average of everyone they have ever
met. The job here is to replace that with one named segment you can actually
reach, described in their words rather than yours.

## When not to use

If you already have paying customers and want to know why they leave, that is
retention work, not discovery. If you want to know how big the market is, use
`solo-market-research`.

## Method

**Step 1: write down who you think it is, then make it narrower twice.**
"Small businesses" is not a segment. "Independent bookkeepers with 5 to 20
clients who still invoice by hand" is. The test: could you name five real
people or find a list of them by Friday? If not, narrow again.

**Step 2: find the pain, not the demographic.** A segment is defined by a
problem that recurs, costs something, and that they have already tried to
solve. Ask what they tried. Existing workarounds are the strongest signal a
market exists, because someone already spent money or effort on the problem.

**Step 3: interview without pitching.** Five to eight conversations gets you
most of the pattern. Ask about the last time the problem happened, in the past
tense, with specifics. Never describe your product until the end, and when you
do, watch whether they ask what it costs.

Questions that work, because they ask about behavior rather than opinion:
- Walk me through the last time this came up. What did you do?
- What did you try before? Why did you stop?
- Who else is involved when this happens?
- What happens if you just leave it broken?
- What did that cost you, in money or hours?

Questions that waste the call, because they invite polite speculation:
"Would you use something that...", "Do you think this is a good idea",
"How much would you pay for...".

**Step 4: read the transcripts for repeats, not highlights.** A thing one
person said eloquently is a quote. A thing five people said badly is a
finding. Count how many independent people raised each theme and keep the
count next to the theme.

**Step 5: write the profile in their words.** Two paragraphs, the segment, the
recurring problem, the current workaround, the cost, and the phrase they use
for it. That phrase becomes your headline later, so record it verbatim.

## Where this goes next

The profile feeds `solo-validate` (will they pay), `solo-pricing` (what the
value metric should be), and `solo-landing-page` (the words above the fold).

## Evidence

A written profile naming one segment, plus interview notes with a per-theme
count of how many independent people raised it. Fewer than five conversations
means `NOT VERIFIED`, and say how many you actually did.
