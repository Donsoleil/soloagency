# Conversion

Conversion work is subtraction. Almost every real win comes from removing a
step, a field, a decision, or a moment of doubt, not from adding persuasion.

## Read first

Get the actual funnel numbers before touching anything. Visit, signup, first
meaningful action, paid. Without stage-by-stage numbers you are guessing at
which step is broken, and you will fix the one that felt wrong to you rather
than the one losing people. If there is no analytics, installing it is the
first task, not an optional prerequisite.

## Find the leak before fixing anything

Compute the drop between each adjacent stage and rank by absolute people lost,
not percentage. A 40% drop late in a small-volume step matters less than a 15%
drop at the top. Fix the biggest absolute loss first.

Then watch five real sessions at that step, or do the step yourself on a phone
on a bad connection. This finds more than any framework, and it routinely finds
something embarrassing and free to fix.

## The friction list, in order of usual payoff

Remove required fields. Every one costs completions. Name and password is
usually enough, and you can ask the rest later, in-product, when they are
already committed.

Delay the account requirement. Let people experience the thing before they
create a login wherever the product allows it.

Remove choices at the moment of decision. A pricing page with two plans
converts better than one with five, because choice at the point of commitment
creates delay and delay creates abandonment.

Show the thing. A short clip of the product doing the job beats a feature list.

Set expectations at every button. "Start free trial, no card needed" outperforms
"Get started" because it answers the question the person is already asking.

## Trial to paid

The number that predicts it is whether they reached first value, the moment the
product did the useful thing once. Find that moment, measure the share who reach
it, and spend your effort getting people there faster. Everything else in
onboarding is decoration.

Card-up-front trials convert a higher share of a smaller group. Card-free
trials fill the top. Neither is correct in general, and for a solo founder
card-up-front is often better because it filters out support load you cannot
absorb.

## Social proof that works

Specific and attributed beats volume. One named customer with a number beats
twenty anonymous five-star quotes. If you have neither, say how many customers
you have honestly, or say nothing. Fabricated or vague proof is detectable and
costs more than it gains.

## A/B tests

At solo-founder traffic, most A/B tests cannot reach significance in any useful
timeframe. Decide the sample size before starting, and if you cannot reach it
within a few weeks, do not run a test: make the better-reasoned change and
watch the trend. Calling a test at 40 conversions is how founders talk
themselves into noise.

## Evidence

The funnel table with absolute numbers per stage, the biggest-loss step named,
the change shipped, and the same table after. A change with no before-number is
`NOT VERIFIED`.
