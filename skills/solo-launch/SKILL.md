# Launch

A launch is not an event that creates demand. It is a moment that concentrates
demand you already built. Founders invert this and then blame the channel.

## The precondition

If you have nobody to tell, you do not have a launch, you have an announcement
into an empty room. Before picking a date, count: people on the list, people
in communities where you are already known and welcome, individuals you can
message personally. If those three numbers are small, spend the next weeks on
`solo-niche-advantage` and come back. This is the single most common reason a
launch flops and it is fully diagnosable in advance.

## Sequence

**Weeks before.** Build the list with a qualifying question so you know who is
on it. Line up the specific people who will vouch, individually asked, never
mass-asked. Make the page, and make sure the price is on it.

**The week before.** Tell the list something is coming and give a date. Write
the messages in advance, per channel, in that channel's register. Prepare the
boring things: a working payment path, a support inbox you will actually read,
and a plan for what happens if something breaks while you are asleep.

**The day.** Go out early in the day for your main audience. Message the
individuals personally, not as a blast. Then spend the day replying to
everything, quickly, as a person. Reply quality on launch day matters more than
the post.

**The week after.** This is the part everyone skips and it is where the
customers are. Follow up with everyone who showed interest and did not buy,
one at a time, asking what stopped them. That list of reasons is worth more
than the launch traffic.

## Product Hunt and similar

Worth it only if your buyers are there, which for most B2B niches they are
not. It buys a spike of technically curious visitors who churn fast. It does
not buy customers in a professional niche. Judge it on whether the audience
matches your segment, not on the traffic number.

## Beta cohorts

Small and paid beats large and free. Five to ten people who paid something
give real feedback because they have standing. A hundred free users give you
support tickets and no signal.

## Relaunching

You can launch repeatedly: new segment, new capability, new channel. A first
launch that flopped is not a verdict, it is usually evidence the precondition
was not met.

## The notebook

Read `~/.soloagency/memory.md` before asking the founder anything it already
answers. When this skill produces a decision, write the entry: what was
decided, why, whether it is one-way, the evidence behind it, and
`Outcome: (open)`. `solo-memory` owns the format.

A decision made and not written down is a decision you will make again from
scratch in four months.

## Evidence

The three precondition counts stated before the date was picked, the messages
per channel, and afterwards the real numbers: reach, signups, paid, plus the
list of stated reasons from people who did not buy. A launch report with
traffic but no paid number is `NOT VERIFIED`.
