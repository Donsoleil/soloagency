# Market research

Two questions only: is there enough money here for one person to live on, and
is the existing competition weak in a way you can exploit. Everything else is
deck filler.

## When not to use

Skip this entirely if you are pre-segment. Sizing an audience you cannot name
produces a number with no meaning. Do `solo-customer-research` first.

## Bottom-up, never top-down

Top-down sizing ("the CRM market is $90B, we only need 0.1%") is how founders
talk themselves into bad ideas. Build it from the bottom instead:

```
reachable customers  ×  realistic annual price  ×  conversion you can defend
= the number that matters
```

Each of the three needs a source. Reachable customers means a list you can
actually name: a directory, an association membership, a subreddit, a trade
body count. Write the source next to the number. A number without a source is
a guess wearing a suit.

Then check it against your own life: at the price you have in mind, how many
customers do you personally need to hit your income floor? If that number is
larger than the segment you can reach, the price is wrong or the segment is.
This is the single most useful calculation in this skill and most founders
never do it.

## Competitor teardown

For each of the three to five real competitors, record only what changes your
decision:

| What | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Price and what unit they charge for | Sets the anchor customers arrive with |
| Who they clearly serve | Shows the segment they cannot serve well |
| What users complain about publicly | Your wedge, in their customers' words |
| How they get customers | Tells you which channel is already paid for |
| How long they have existed | Old and unloved is a better target than new and loved |

Read the one and two star reviews, the support forum, and the churn complaints
on social. Public complaints about an incumbent are the cheapest market
research that exists.

## Reading the result

Crowded with happy customers is the worst outcome, worse than empty. Crowded
with frustrated customers is the best. Empty usually means no money, not
undiscovered opportunity, and the burden of proof is on you to show otherwise.

## Evidence

The bottom-up calculation with a cited source per input, the customers-needed-
to-live number, and a teardown table with at least three named competitors and
real quoted complaints. Uncited inputs are `NOT VERIFIED`.
